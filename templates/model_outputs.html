Exploratory Data Analysis

Understanding the dataset before model training — patterns, distributions, and relationships between features and car price.

Price Distribution

Car Price Distribution

The histogram shows the distribution of car prices across the 10,000-row dataset. Prices range from approximately $2,000 to $18,000 with a roughly bell-shaped distribution centred around $8,000–$10,000.

💡 Key Insight: The near-normal price distribution means the dataset is well-balanced with no extreme skew, which is favourable for regression model training.
Brand Analysis

Average Price by Brand

Mean prices are relatively close across all 10 brands ($8,665–$9,016). Chevrolet leads at $9,016, while Honda is slightly lower at $8,666. The narrow range suggests brand alone is not a dominant price driver.

💡 Key Insight: Brand has a modest effect on price; Year and Mileage are stronger determinants (confirmed by feature importance).
Fuel Type Analysis

Average Price by Fuel Type

Electric vehicles command the highest average price ($10,032), followed by Hybrid ($9,113), Diesel ($8,117), and Petrol ($8,071).

💡 Key Insight: Electric & Hybrid vehicles are priced ~$1,000–$2,000 higher than combustion-engine cars — fuel type is a meaningful price signal.
Transmission Analysis

Average Price by Transmission

Automatic vehicles average $9,938 — significantly higher than Manual ($8,363) and Semi-Automatic ($8,264). The $1,575 gap between Automatic and Manual is notable.

💡 Key Insight: Automatic transmission commands a ~19% premium over manual, reflecting market preference and modern vehicle trends.
Doors Analysis

Average Price by Number of Doors

All door configurations (2–5) show similar average prices ($8,746–$8,929), with minimal variation. 2-door vehicles are marginally higher at $8,929.

💡 Key Insight: Door count has minimal direct impact on price — confirming its low feature importance ranking in the trained model.
Model Analysis

Average Price by Car Model (30 Models)

Across all 30 models, average prices range from ~$8,581 (BMW X5) to ~$9,099 (Volkswagen Tiguan). The spread is narrow, suggesting similar price ranges across models within this dataset.

💡 Key Insight: Car model has limited standalone impact — model-specific pricing is better captured in combination with year, mileage and engine size.
Correlation Analysis

Feature Correlation Heatmap

The heatmap shows pairwise correlations between numeric features and price. Year has the strongest positive correlation with price (+0.66). Mileage has a strong negative correlation (-0.55). Engine Size has a moderate positive correlation (+0.36).

Year → Price: +0.66
Mileage → Price: −0.55
Engine Size → Price: +0.36
💡 Key Insight: Newer cars are worth more; higher mileage reduces value. These strong linear correlations explain why linear models also performed well in evaluation.

Model Evaluation

11 regression models were trained and evaluated on the same dataset. Results are compared across RMSE, MAE, MSE, R², and execution time.

RMSE Comparison

Model Performance — RMSE

Root Mean Squared Error (lower is better). Lasso Regression (our model) achieves excellent RMSE (~120) due to the strong linear relationship between Year/Mileage and Price. KNN performs worst (~1,250). Among ensemble/boosting models, LightGBM and CatBoost perform competitively (~260–280).

💡 Key Insight: Lasso Regression exploits the strong linear correlation between Year/Mileage and Price. Its low RMSE demonstrates excellent accuracy for this particular regression problem.
R² Score Comparison

Model Performance — R² Score

R² Score (higher is better, max = 1.0). Linear models including Lasso achieve near-perfect R² (~1.0). KNN is the weakest (~0.84). CatBoost, XGBoost, and LightGBM all achieve ~0.99, confirming excellent fit on the test set.

💡 Key Insight: Lasso's R² of ~1.0 means it explains nearly 100% of the variance in car prices — an excellent result for a real-world regression problem.
MAE Comparison

Model Performance — MAE

Mean Absolute Error (lower is better). Lasso Regression achieves the lowest MAE (~30), meaning predictions are on average $30 away from the true price — an excellent result for used car pricing.

💡 Key Insight: An average error of ~$30 on prices ranging $2,000–$18,000 represents less than 1% average error for Lasso, demonstrating outstanding prediction accuracy.
MSE Comparison

Model Performance — MSE

Mean Squared Error penalizes large errors more heavily than MAE. KNN shows the highest MSE (~1.54M). Lasso Regression achieves one of the lowest MSE values, competing favorably with ensemble models.

💡 Key Insight: Lasso's low MSE demonstrates its ability to avoid large individual prediction errors, which is critical for reliable and trustworthy car price estimates.
Training Efficiency

Model Training Execution Times

Training time comparison across all 11 models. Random Forest is the slowest (~11 seconds). Lasso Regression trains near-instantly (<0.05 seconds) — the fastest among all models while maintaining excellent prediction quality.

LR: ~0.05s
RF: ~11s
Lasso: ~0.05s
💡 Key Insight: Lasso offers an unbeatable speed-to-accuracy ratio. It trains in ~0.05 seconds while achieving R² near 1.0 — making it ideal for production deployment.
